# Drawing Roadmap v2: Circles & Lines → Realism, Manga, Comics & Impact Effects
**A day-by-day plan with end-of-day checks, weekly tests and stage checkpoints. Version 2: audited against live sources (see Section 0).**

Destination: you can look at (or imagine) a scene, construct it, light it, give it emotion, add effects (smoke, tears, water, waterfalls, explosions, impact frames) and tell it as a comic sequence.

- **Length:** 54 weeks core (~12 months at ~2 hrs/day, 6 days + 1 test/review day), then ongoing capstone work. **Checkpoints, not the calendar, decide when you advance.** If you fail a Sunday test, repeat that week.
- **Method:** 70% drawing, 30% watching. Never watch a lesson without drawing along.

### Things v1 missed (now added, stage by stage, below)
1. **A self-evaluation toolkit.** Research on observational drawing points to three error sources: perception, motor control, and misjudging your own drawing. Beginners usually can't see their own errors, so Stage 0 now teaches mirror-flip, overlay, squint and grid checks.
2. **Learning-habit and ergonomics training** (Ctrl+Paint has whole sections on both).
3. **Free official manga tooling and practice** (JUMP PAINT's Challenge JUMP).
4. **Real manga production specs** (manuscript size, DPI, bleed, moiré), which matter for Stage 10.
5. **Direct sources for the effects you asked about** (water, smoke, fire, explosions, impact), including exact tutorials.
6. **Gesture, head and figure-construction courses** that exist on Proko but were missing.
7. **A route to publish/submit** your finished work, and a critique loop at every stage.

### Evidence notes (so you can trust the method)
- A 16-week observational drawing course produced measurable skill gains in a study of 45 participants, so structured observational practice works.
- Imaging work links drawing skill to fine-motor and procedural-memory structures, which supports lots of repeated, deliberate mark-making (Drawabox-style) as well as perception work.
- The popular "right-brain/left-brain" explanation from *Drawing on the Right Side of the Brain* is not supported by neuroscience. Its exercises (contour, negative space) are still useful, but the reason is trained perception and measuring, not switching hemispheres. I do not rely on that theory anywhere.

## 3. The daily template (120 min, Mon–Sat)

| Min | Block | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | **Warm-up** | Ghosted lines, ellipses, or 30-sec gestures. |
| 25 | **Learn** | Watch/read the day's resource *with pencil in hand*. Pause and draw along. |
| 35 | **Practice** | The day's exercise. Quantity with attention, not speed. |
| 25 | **Bridge (apply yesterday)** | Redraw yesterday's subject using today's concept, or combine both in one drawing. Then **hide the reference and redraw from memory.** |
| 15 | **End-of-day check + log** | Do the check in the table, write 3 mistakes + 1 fix in your Error Log. Photograph the page. |

**Sunday:** 60 min redo-weakest-items, 30 min weekly test, 30 min review of the week's Error Log. Rest or free-draw after (the "50% rule": half your total time should be drawing for fun).

**Six-beat method for any new subject** (smoke, hands, waterfall...): Observe → Deconstruct (basic forms) → Understand (why it behaves so) → Reconstruct (simple shapes first) → Stylize (manga/comic version) → Test (from memory, then a never-seen variation).

## 4. Scoring system (use every Sunday/checkpoint)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Can't do it |
| 1 | Understand it, execution poor |
| 2 | Can do it only with heavy reference/correction |
| 3 | Independent and consistent |
| 4 | Accurate, adaptable, and I can explain the principle |

Checkpoint pass: **12/16** (four skills scored out of 4) with no skill below 2. Final portfolio: **14/16**. Be honest; ask a friend or an online community (r/learnart, r/ArtCrit, Drawabox's subreddit) to critique, since you can't see your own blind spots.

**Error Log format (one line per mistake):**
`Date | Drawing | Mistake | Cause (e.g., "didn't check angle") | Fix | Re-tested on (date)`

## 4b. How to read the "Why this?" column

Every day in the roadmap now says **what it builds** and **what you will actually feel while drawing it**. Use it three ways:
1. **Before you draw:** read the Why so you know what you're training (accuracy, hand control, spatial thinking, emotion, etc.).
2. **While you draw:** look for the sensation described (e.g., "you'll feel lines land where you aimed"). If you don't feel it, repeat the exercise more slowly.
3. **After you draw:** use the end-of-day check to see whether the skill showed up. If not, log it in your Error Log.

### The big picture: why each stage exists
| Stage | Why it exists | What you'll experience | What it unlocks later |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0: Observation baseline | Your brain draws symbols (a stored 'cup', 'eye'). Measuring trains you to draw what's really there; the baseline proves progress. | Frustration, then small 'aha' moments when a measured drawing suddenly looks right. | Every realistic and manga drawing depends on seeing accurately. |
| 1: Lines, ellipses, shapes | Hand control. If you can't place a line where you intend, nothing built from lines works. | Boring but quick gains in accuracy; confident strokes replace scratchy ones. | Smooth curves for gesture, hair, smoke, clothes; ellipses for cylinders, limbs and heads. |
| 2: 3D forms and perspective | Converts flat shapes into volumes in space, and lets you draw from imagination instead of copying. | Mental rotation effort; frustration with rotated boxes, then a sense that lines 'want' to converge. | Backgrounds, camera angles, foreshortened fists, and every constructed character. |
| 3: Observation and realism | Applies construction to real objects so you can reproduce anything accurately. | Slower, more careful drawing; your errors become measurable. | Portraits, animals, and the reference-to-invention method. |
| 4: Light, shadow, materials | Makes forms look solid; teaches how surfaces react to light. | Spheres 'pop' off the page; you start seeing light on everything. | Shading, manga tones, glass and water, tears, smoke lighting. |
| 5: Gesture and anatomy | Gives figures life, weight and believable structure. | Quick poses feel loose; anatomy first feels like memorisation, then starts explaining what you see. | Action poses, fights, emotional body language. |
| 6: Heads, faces, expressions | Faces carry emotion; construction lets you rotate and stylise them safely. | Features stop floating on the face and start wrapping around it. | Manga faces, emotional scenes, portraits. |
| 7: Realism integration | Combines all skills in finished pieces and tests drawing from understanding. | Long sessions; you'll see where weaknesses show up in real work. | Confidence and a portfolio baseline. |
| 8: Manga and characters | Selective exaggeration built on real structure. | Fun, faster results; a risk of dropping fundamentals if not balanced. | Original characters and consistent designs. |
| 9: Effects and impact | Effects have structure and logic; seeing why smoke, water or impact behaves that way lets you stylise it. | You'll start seeing shapes in smoke, water and explosions instead of 'scribbles'. | Impactful action panels, atmosphere, emotional symbolism. |
| 10: Comic storytelling | A drawing shows a moment; a comic controls time and attention. | Thumbnails feel messy at first, then you'll notice you can plan before drawing. | Readable pages, pacing, finished manga. |
| 11: Capstone | Proves you can create independently, not just follow tutorials. | Pressure and ownership; every decision is yours. | An original portfolio piece and a repeatable workflow. |

**Why week 1's eye, sphere, figure, building and crying face?** Together they test five core skills (detail observation, light/form, gesture, perspective, emotion). You will probably draw them poorly, and that's the point: you'll redraw them every 4 weeks (and at each stage checkpoint) to see exactly what each stage added.

**Why draw lines and ellipses when you want manga?** Manga is built from confident lines (inking), curves (hair, cloth, smoke) and 3D forms (heads, bodies, backgrounds). Without line control you ink shakily; without ellipses and boxes you can't rotate a head or draw a room from a new angle.

# 5. THE ROADMAP

# STAGE 0: Baseline & Observation (Week 1)

### Week 1: Measure where you are, train your eyes
**Why this week/stage exists:** Build an honest starting point, and replace 'drawing the symbol I remember' with 'drawing what is actually in front of me'. Everything later depends on this habit.

| Day | Learn | Practice | Bridge (apply earlier learning) | End-of-day check | Why this? (what it builds, what you'll feel) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | No tutorial | **Baseline test, no help:** realistic eye, shaded sphere, action figure, building from a corner, a person crying. Date and keep all 5. | n/a | All five saved and dated. | Gives you a before-picture and exposes your habits (symbol-style eyes, flat spheres). Expect it to feel bad; that discomfort is the data you will compare against at month 6 and 12. |
| Tue | CP: visual measuring + contour | Draw bottle, cup, shoe from life; blind contour ×5 | Redraw Monday's sphere from life with a real ball | Can you name 3 proportion errors? | Your brain stores a symbol of 'cup'. Measuring forces you to draw THIS cup's proportions. It feels slow and awkward because you are overriding a lifelong shortcut. |
| Wed | CP: negative space | Draw a chair and a plant using only the *gaps* | Draw Tuesday's cup via negative space | Does the gap-drawing match the real object when overlaid in a photo? | Gaps between objects have no name, so symbol memory can't interfere. If the gaps are right, the objects are right. You'll get an 'aha' when a rough chair suddenly looks correct. |
| Thu | CP: comparative measurement/angles | Shoe, book, spoon: measure width:height, angles with a pencil held at arm's length | Redraw Wednesday's chair with measured proportions | 3 ratios correct within ~10%. | Turns 'looks about right' into numbers (ratios, angles). You'll feel less guessing and more checking; this is the start of accuracy. |
| Fri | DB Lesson 1 intro: ghosting, confidence | 2 pages of superimposed lines (see Wk2) | Use confident lines to redraw Thursday's book | No "hairy" re-stroked lines. | Starts hand control: one committed stroke instead of scratchy corrections. You'll feel the difference between a ghosted confident line and a hesitant one. |
| Sat | Set up | Install Krita; set up notebook + Error Log; redraw bottle/cup/shoe | Compare with Tuesday's | Side-by-side: what improved? | Removes friction (tools, notebook, error log) so the habit survives. Redrawing the same three objects shows real progress after only five days. |
| **Sun** | **TEST 0: Observation** | Draw 5 everyday objects in one composition, no shading, from life | n/a | **Pass = 4/5:** objects recognizable; relative sizes right; angles right; negative spaces right; no decoration hiding errors. | A scored gate so you advance on evidence, not mood. Expect to spot errors on Sunday that you couldn't see on Tuesday; that is the observing skill growing. |

### Stage 0 upgrade pack (added in v2)
**Add these to Week 1 (as extra 15-minute blocks, or on Saturday):**
- **Self-evaluation toolkit (new, do this after every drawing from now on):** (1) *Flip it*: mirror the image (Krita: Image → Flip, or hold it to a mirror); errors jump out. (2) *Overlay*: photograph your drawing and your reference at the same angle and overlay at 50% opacity. (3) *Squint test* for value and silhouette. (4) *Step back* 2 m or view at thumbnail size. (5) *Sleep on it*: re-check the next day. (6) *Ask for critique* (Drawabox reviews, Line of Action forum Critique section, r/learnart).
- **Mindset and habit videos (CPV, free):** *Being a Beginner*, *Expectation vs. Reality*, *Start Anywhere*, *The Drawing Habit*, *Targeted Learning pt. 1 and 2*, *What is a "study"?*, *Give Yourself Assignments*. Also read **Drawabox Lesson 0** (mindset and the 50% rule).
- **Ergonomics (CPV):** *Ergonomics and Hardware*, *How to Hold a Stylus*. Rule of thumb *(my knowledge)*: a 5-minute break every 45 minutes, draw long strokes from the shoulder rather than gripping tight with the fingers, and stop if you feel pain or tingling; repetitive strain in drawing hands is real.
- **Reference library:** create folders (people, hands, faces, animals, smoke, water, fire, explosions, architecture, fabric). Use free-license photos (pexels, unsplash, pixabay) or your own photos, so you can legally reuse and share your studies.
- **New test, Test 0b (Sunday):** after your five-object test, flip it, overlay it and list 5 specific errors you *found yourself*. Score 0–4 on "can I find my own mistakes?" This skill predicts how fast you'll improve.

# STAGE 1: Lines, Ellipses, Shapes (Weeks 2–5)
*Goal: deliberate marks. Start of the "circles and lines" foundation.*

### Week 2: Line control
**Why this week/stage exists:** Hand control. Every figure, face and effect later is made of strokes you must place where you intended, not 'somewhere near'.

| Day | Learn | Practice | Bridge | End-of-day check | Why this? (what it builds, what you'll feel) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | DB L1: Superimposed Lines | 3 pages: 50 lines each, start→end points marked | n/a | ≥80% lines land within ~3 mm of the end mark. | Trains planning a stroke between two points. You'll notice lines land where you aimed instead of drifting. |
| Tue | DB L1: Ghosted Lines & Planes | 2 pages ghosted lines + 1 page ghosted planes | Superimposed lines warm-up first | Lines are single strokes, no scratching. | Ghosting rehearses the arm motion before ink; the same pre-motion is used later for gesture and perspective. You'll feel a pause-then-commit rhythm. |
| Wed | DB L1: Arcs / C- and S-curves | 2 pages of curves between marks; draw S-curve spine "snakes" | Draw a leaf and a banana outline with one stroke per curve | Curves flow, no flat spots. | C- and S-curves are the skeleton of gesture, hair, cloth and smoke. You'll feel smooth shoulder-driven curves versus wobbly wrist ones. |
| Thu | Line weight & tapered strokes (PR Drawing Basics: line) | Same mug drawn thin / thick / tapered | Use Wednesday's curves to draw the mug | You can state what each weight communicates (depth, emphasis). | Line weight creates depth and emphasis; manga inkers use it to separate foreground from background. A flat mug visibly gains volume from thickness changes alone. |
| Fri | Hatching & cross-hatching (YT: "hatching cross-hatching drawing basics") | 6 patches of even hatching, gradients | Hatch the mug from Thursday | Gradient is smooth, spacing even. | Hatching is your first value tool and becomes shading and screentone later. You'll find that even spacing is harder than it looks. |
| Sat | Review | Redo the weakest two exercises | n/a | Photo-compare to Monday. | Redoing the weakest exercise targets your bottleneck, not your comfort zone. |
| **Sun** | **TEST 1A: Line control** | 30 straight lines, 20 curves, 20 parallels, 20 angled | n/a | **Pass:** deliberate, accurate, consistent. Score 0–4. | Checks the skill is repeatable under test conditions, not a lucky page. |

### Week 3: Ellipses
**Why this week/stage exists:** Ellipses are circles seen at an angle. Cylinders, limbs, heads, cups and wheels are all ellipses, so smooth ellipses are the gateway from flat shapes to 3D.

| Day | Learn | Practice | Bridge | End-of-day check | Why this? (what it builds, what you'll feel) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | DB L1: Ghosted Ellipses | 2 pages of ellipses in various widths | Warm-up lines | Smooth, closed, no overlapping stutter. | Builds the arm motion for smooth closed curves. Wobble means you're drawing from the wrist; you'll feel the whirling rhythm come when you use the shoulder. |
| Tue | DB L1: Table of Ellipses | Full table (ellipses in squares at graded degrees) | Draw ellipses in Monday's style first | Degree gradient is visible. | Shows how an ellipse narrows or widens with viewing angle: the visual rule behind every circular opening. You'll start spotting ellipses on real objects. |
| Wed | DB L1: Funnels (ellipses sharing one minor axis); drawing through | Ellipses with central axes, overlapped | Combine into a stack of 5 discs | Minor axis perpendicular to major. | Teaches that ellipses on a cylinder share one axis. This later fixes wobbly bottles, arms and legs. You'll feel the logic 'axis first, then ellipse'. |
| Thu | Ellipses in planes (DB L1) | 20 ellipses inside rectangles/planes touching all 4 sides | Add a cylinder outline using Wed's axis method | Tangent points touch the edges. | Fitting an ellipse into a plane trains placement and tangency, the same precision needed for wheels in perspective. |
| Fri | Ellipses → real objects | Draw 5 bottles/cups/bowls from life using ellipses | Reuse Tuesday's table as a reference | Openings are believable. | Moves skill from exercise to observation so it transfers; you'll start seeing cups and bottles as stacked ellipses. |
| Sat | Review | Redo worst ellipse exercise | n/a | Count: how many of 30 are "good"? Aim 20+. | Fixes the worst of the week's ellipses while the motion is still fresh. |
| **Sun** | **TEST 1B: Ellipse control** | 30 varied ellipses, 10 with axes, 10 inside rectangles | n/a | **Pass:** reasonably smooth, consistent orientation. | Confirms smoothness and consistent orientation, not just effort. |

### Week 4: 2D shapes → combined shapes
**Why this week/stage exists:** Learn to build things by combining simple shapes, the first step toward constructing animals, faces and bodies instead of copying outlines.

| Day | Learn | Practice | Bridge | End-of-day check | Why this? (what it builds, what you'll feel) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | PR Drawing Basics: shapes | 15 shapes (circle, square, rectangle, triangle, polygon, ellipse) at different sizes | Warm-up ellipses | Shapes are even, corners crisp. | Clean basic shapes are your alphabet. You'll notice crisp corners and even sizes take deliberate effort. |
| Tue | Shape combination (CP: "shapes" videos) | Build an animal from circles + triangles | Use Monday's shapes | Animal reads clearly. | Combining shapes teaches you to see structure inside a subject. You'll feel an animal 'click' once you see it as circles and triangles. |
| Wed | Silhouette | Draw 10 silhouettes (everyday objects) | Turn Tuesday's animal into a filled silhouette | Can a friend identify each silhouette? | Silhouette is how readers recognise a character or object at a glance, which matters hugely in manga. You'll see which shapes carry the identity. |
| Thu | Organic/irregular shapes | Leaves, rocks, clouds as shape masses | Combine with Wednesday's silhouettes | Shape is varied, not symmetrical. | Real forms are irregular; this stops you drawing everything symmetrical and stiff. Rocks and clouds feel looser. |
| Fri | Shape-based character | Simple character from shapes | Apply Tuesday's method | Can you see which shape is each body part? | Applies shape-building to a character, previewing Stage 8. You'll see how body parts map to shapes. |
| Sat | Review | Redraw the weakest piece | n/a | n/a | Re-draw the weakest piece so mistakes get corrected, not repeated. |
| **Sun** | **TEST 1C: Shape construction** | Animal, vehicle OR face, using only circles, ellipses, triangles, rectangles | n/a | Proportions identifiable, shapes combined without losing structure. | Tests whether you can build a subject from shapes without losing proportion. |

### Week 5: Observation meets shape + STAGE 1 CHECKPOINT
**Why this week/stage exists:** Combine seeing (Week 1) with shape-building (Week 4): measure first, then build, then check. This is the skill you'll use for every reference you ever draw.

| Day | Learn | Practice | Bridge | End-of-day check | Why this? (what it builds, what you'll feel) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Envelope method (YT: "envelope drawing method") | Boil 5 objects to a simple polygon, then refine | Shape combos | Polygon matches outer boundary. | Reducing an object to an outer polygon locks proportion before detail. You'll feel your drawings stop collapsing. |
| Tue | Plumb lines & alignment | 3 objects with vertical/horizontal alignment checks | Envelope first | Landmarks align when checked. | Plumb lines check alignment objectively. You'll catch tilted bottles and misplaced features your eye used to forgive. |
| Wed | Cup, bottle, shoe from life | 3 drawings, full measuring | Use envelope + plumb | Compare to photo overlay. | Applies the tools to real objects; you'll see how much more accurate you are than in Week 1. |
| Thu | Negative space on a complex object (bike, chair) | 2 drawings | Wednesday's measuring | Gaps right? | Negative space on a complex object (bike, chair) proves the method scales. You'll feel it handle shapes you couldn't draw before. |
| Fri | Unfamiliar object test | Draw an object you haven't drawn before, 20 min, no tutorial | n/a | Did you measure or "symbolize"? | Tests whether you measure or fall back on symbols when nobody is helping you. |
| Sat | Compile sketchbook | Assemble best pages | n/a | n/a | Compiling lets you see growth over five weeks, which keeps motivation honest. |
| **Sun** | **CHECKPOINT 1** | Page of lines, 30 ellipses, 15 shapes, cup + bottle + shoe from observation, one negative-space drawing | n/a | **Pass:** confident lines; smooth ellipses; believable proportions; you can find and fix your own mistakes. **Key question: can you draw an unfamiliar object by measuring, not from a memorized symbol?** | Stage gate: don't move into 3D until you can measure real objects. Skipping this makes later stages harder. |

### Stage 1 upgrade pack (added in v2)

**New method tips for effects** *(my knowledge, apply them with your references)*:
- **Study real footage:** find slow-motion clips of water splashes, smoke, fire, impacts; pause frames and draw *shapes and directions*, not details.
- **Impact timing:** use the classic animation beats *(anticipation → contact → follow-through)* in your thumbnails for every punch and explosion.
- **Reserve your blackest blacks and whitest whites** for the impact point; keep surrounding values mid-range so the impact pops.
- **Let effects break panel borders** only at moments of peak action; contained effects feel contained.
- **Emanata and symbols** (sweat drops, anger marks, blush lines) are part of the effects vocabulary. Make a personal "effects sheet" and add one item per week.
- **Test 9f (Sunday of Week 44 add-on):** redraw one of your effects *without* looking at any reference, then with it. List what you forgot.

### Stage 10 upgrade pack (added in v2): production specs and publishing route
**Manuscript specs (verified):**
- Standard professional manuscript is **B4 paper with a basic (inner) frame of 180 mm wide × 270 mm tall** (e.g., Kodansha's Afternoon contest requires this). Keep text and important faces inside the inner frame; art may bleed past it to the trim.
- **Black-and-white manuscripts: 600 dpi** (Clip Studio calls 600 the standard; 1200 is almost never used). **Color: 350 dpi.** Pure B/W manuscripts must be black or white only. Mixing anti-aliasing and screentones causes **moiré**; read CSP TIPS *How to Create and Export Digital Comic Manuscripts Without Moiré* (article 1019), *Digital manuscript settings in Clip Studio Paint* (article 7673) and *Basics of creating a manga manuscript* (article 733).
- For small-press print, pixiv FACTORY examples use B5 (182×257 mm finished size, 3 mm bleed, 600 dpi canvas); always check your printer's template.
**Workflow sources:** Whyt Manga *Making Manga in Clip Studio Paint, Full Breakdown* and *Manga Storyboards from Script*; MediBang's *Manga Tutorial for Beginners* (making the *Name*/rough layout, frames, publishing).
**Tools:** CSP **EX** for multi-page projects (it has the *Story* feature for a whole book); free route: **JUMP PAINT** or **Krita 5.3's Comic Panel tool**. Reading direction: Japanese manga reads right-to-left; Western comics left-to-right; decide at the start.
**Format choice (new):** decide whether your story is **paged manga** or **vertical-scroll Webtoon** before thumbnails; pacing, panel size and effect lines differ (CSP TIPS has Webtoon-specific effects tutorials).
**Where to practice/submit (new):** JUMP PAINT's *Challenge JUMP* pages; Shueisha's **MANGA Plus Creators** (free, accepts submissions in English and Spanish; JUMP PAINT can submit directly). Check each platform's rules and current contests before you submit.
**Listening:** *Comic Lab* podcast was recommended by a learner list I found; check it for craft talk.
**New test 10f:** print one page at actual B4 inner-frame size and check that text is readable from 60 cm, and that tones don't moiré.

# 6. Weekly & monthly rules that keep you moving

1. **Always finish the Sunday test.** If you score below 2 on any skill, repeat the week.
2. **Every 4 weeks:** redraw your baseline 5 drawings from Week 1 and compare. Photograph both.
3. **Every stage:** keep 5 best pieces in a portfolio folder; ask for critique online.
4. **Bridge rule:** every day starts with a 5-minute redraw (from memory) of yesterday's subject. If you can't, you didn't learn it yet. Repeat it.
5. **Memory test:** at the end of each week, hide your references and draw 3 things from that week. The ones that fail go into next week's warm-ups.
6. **50% rule:** half of your time should be drawing things you like (manga characters, fanart, scenes). Exercises serve the work you care about.
7. **Avoid the common traps:** endless circle drills without 3D; practising only anime faces; copying the same reference repeatedly; starting details too early; memorizing muscle names without understanding form; watching tutorials passively; skipping perspective; drawing effects without studying real phenomena.
